# Learning at Willow Cove

The family-lodge prototype opens with a short adult haul. Players can repeat the trip, excavate the east channel, sleep through the first water drop, undo an experiment or open the construction sandbox. The three-night challenge remains available separately. The sequence below describes the broader design, including future site scouting and free movement.

## The first trip

Open at water level beside the family lodge. An adult swims in with a leafy branch, climbs out at a muddy lip and drags it the last few lengths. Let the player move immediately. Give one invitation: “Want to bring one home?” Point out the grove only if the player asks for a hint. Start with enough food for this first night so exploring costs time without ending the lesson.

| Moment | What the player can try | What the creek shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Follow the adult | Tap the grove, collect a branch and choose a way home. | The beaver swims with the branch afloat, then slows where it has to drag it. The branch appears at the lodge. |
| Change the trip | Inspect the muddy lip. Try a short cut, preview a higher crest, or keep using the bank. Offer Undo at the first landscape edit. | A cut changes the bed. A raised crest moves the shoreline. On the next haul the player sees exactly where swimming becomes possible. |
| See what lasts | Settle in when ready. Revisit the landing after the water falls overnight. | Yesterday’s shoreline remains visible on the bank. The same cut may now be shallow. The lodge doorway gives a second place to look for the effect of falling water. |
| Choose a home | Follow the creek beyond the family pond. Inspect a narrow crossing and a deep bank. Come home or choose a site. | The narrow crossing suggests a pond the player could build. The deep bank offers shelter with water already at its entrance. |

## How guidance fades

Show the movement prompt when control begins, the hauling prompt at a reachable branch, and the build preview at a site the player inspects. Dismiss a prompt when its action is understood. Keep a replayable control hint available. Players can leave early, stay to experiment or revisit the family lodge. Departure has no required upgrades or perfect-night score.

Use the first trip to teach moving and hauling. Use the next to show why building matters. Save full dam construction, shelter placement and the cost of a poor site for the founding chapter. A single authored adult trip can demonstrate a route and reuse an improved landing without introducing a family simulation.

## First playtest

Give a first-time player the creek without narrating the solution. Observe whether they can bring one branch home, point to the awkward part of the trip, and predict where a landscape change will help. After the overnight drop, ask where they would put their own home and what they saw that led them there. Record requests for help and voluntary repeat trips. Completion time alone cannot tell whether the lesson transferred.
