## Three layers of progression
1. **Individual/family expertise:** River Sense and construction/foraging practice. Per-scenario or per-beaver; never changes physical laws.
2. **Player journal and campaign:** observations, completed challenges and new scenario access persist; not automatic inherited competence.
3. **Watershed state:** water, vegetation, food access and habitat indicators respond to choices. This is *not* a purchased skill tree.

## Initial capability branches
| Branch | Basic capability | Later improvement | MVP? |
|---|---|---|---|
| River Sense | Observe depth/current and inspect clues | Explain flood risk and compare sites | Yes, first slice |
| Shelter craft | Place lodge or excavate den | Reinforcement and complex bank lodge | Basic only |
| Construction | Build/repair simple dam | Complex segments, maintenance efficiencies | Water slice only |
| Foraging | Gather branches and food | Selective harvesting and winter planning | Basic only |
| Family coordination | Assign one simple work action | Parallel work, kits and dispersal | Later |

Unlock by using systems and reviewing outcomes, not generic XP spending. Do not lock basic tools behind grind.

## Habitat consequences
An intervention can change upstream water coverage, submerged access, willow availability, wetland plant suitability and habitat connectivity. Positive effects for one indicator can be negative for another. A free-flowing stream with intact vegetation may already satisfy ecological goals; sometimes **not building** is best.

### Scenario archetypes
- **Establish a wetland:** degraded or low-wetland reach where water retention can help; sparse initial resources, visible transformation.
- **Enrich an established stream:** existing wildlife and vegetation; focus on tradeoffs and minimizing disruption.
- **Deep/variable river:** dam infeasible; shelter assessment and seasonal resilience.
- **Unsuitable reach:** recognize risk and relocate rather than force construction.

These are design categories, not assertions that all undammed streams require restoration.

## Campaign outline (later, not MVP)
First Creek → Willow Bend → Riverbank Refuge → Spring Flood → Rocky Gorge → Living Valley. Each chapter adds one reasoning variable, then recombines earlier skills. Optional mastery objectives should reward good decisions, not maximize one habitat metric.

## Balance rules
- One core new concept per introductory chapter.
- At least two viable strategies when a chapter tests choice; some unsuitable locations should be clearly signaled.
- Environmental effects have understandable delays and no unexplained random penalties.
- Seasonal disasters are telegraphed in introductory mode.
- No irreversible failure spiral; repair/relocation remains possible.
- Optional ecology journal labels simplifications and uncertainty.

## Open design choices
Whether progression applies to individual beavers or the family; exact skill unlock pacing; whether ecological indicators are numerical or qualitative; how winter stores and vegetation regrowth interact. Validate with a playable slice before fixing a full tree.
