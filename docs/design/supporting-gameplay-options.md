# Supporting gameplay gaps — resolution options

**Date:** 2026-11-09. **Status:** design options, not selected scope or playtest findings. Companion to [core options](riverbank-refuge-resolution-options.md) and the [review](../reviews/gameplay-design-review.md).

These cover G11–G14 and G16–G21. G15 is covered in the core options. Each recommendation states the smallest useful next document rather than requiring all later features in Riverbank Refuge.

## Opening and help — G11

**Needed:** first-minute actions, objective framing, optional hints, and an end-to-end paper UI sequence.

| Option | Benefit | Cost / risk |
|---|---|---|
| Guided inspection tour before building | Predictable teaching order | Reading/clicking can delay agency |
| Contextual help during a reversible plan | Early manipulation with feedback; player chooses what to inspect | Needs clear selection and undo states |
| Unguided arrival with optional hint button | Strong discovery and autonomy | Some first-time players may not know what matters |

**Recommend contextual help**, with optional observation → inference → action hints. Begin with a visible need and a harmless placement preview, then explain the clue relevant to that preview. Do not require all clues to be clicked before acting if the player already understands them.

**Artifact:** first-minute storyboard and three-level hint examples for flood exposure and low-water access. Test whether the opening supports action rather than merely counting taps.

## Family presence and feedback — G12

**Needed:** how the family makes home safety emotionally legible without individual-agent simulation.

| Option | Benefit | Cost / risk |
|---|---|---|
| Family as static portrait/status panel | Cheap and clear | Weak connection to the river |
| Small contextual family scenes | Arrival, carrying supplies, using home/route make outcomes tangible | Requires feedback timing and accessible alternatives |
| Individual needs, schedules and personalities | Rich attachment and emergent behavior | Expands into colony simulation |

**Recommend small contextual scenes.** Pair each with a state change and plain-language explanation; allow skipping motion. Expressive celebration can accompany safe access, but cannot replace the actual safety indicator.

**Artifact:** inspect/build/flood/success action → world change → family reaction table. Later art choices are not needed to write it.

## Transfer and replay — G13

**Needed:** distinguish remembering Riverbank Refuge from applying its principles.

| Option | Benefit | Cost / risk |
|---|---|---|
| Replay identical map for fewer repairs | Easy mastery goal | Mostly tests memory and optimization |
| Second handcrafted reach with changed tradeoffs | Tests transfer without new systems | Requires another small authored scenario |
| Procedural sites/weather | Broad variation | Balancing and fairness become much harder |

**Recommend a second paper reach.** Retain the same physical rules but make a sheltered lodge attractive and a visibly unstable bank unsuitable. Change evidence with conditions, not hidden rules. No requirement for endless replay of a tutorial.

**Artifact:** contrasting site table, intended inference, two plausible choices and explanation of what transfers. Record voluntary replay separately from transfer success.

## Difficulty and accessibility — G14

**Needed:** what can become harder while preserving readable evidence and non-reflex play.

| Option | Benefit | Cost / risk |
|---|---|---|
| Same rules, adjustable support | Ages and reading levels can share a scenario | Does not itself add strategic depth |
| Optional work/material challenge | Stronger planning with familiar rules | Budget can eliminate one strategy or recovery |
| More uncertainty and hidden information | Greater anticipation | Can weaken fairness and cause explanation |

**Recommend one standard ruleset with optional support first.** Later opt-in challenge budgets can retain the same evidence and physical rules, provided at least two plans and a recovery path still exist. Support choices do not reduce base completion credit.

**Artifact:** fixed fairness rules, adjustable support list, and later challenge budget checks. Test child/adult comprehension separately without assuming every age group needs different rules.

## Ecology as a choice — G16

**Needed:** a causal chain with a visible delay, competing use and player response.

| Option | Benefit | Cost / risk |
|---|---|---|
| Visual habitat response only | Landscape feels alive | Does not create a strategic choice |
| One vegetation patch has competing uses | Harvest now versus later food/cover connects existing systems | Needs timing and eligibility rules |
| Multi-species habitat simulation | Richer ecological interactions | Much larger design and factual burden |

**Recommend visual context for A; test one competing-use patch for integration.** Do not retrofit a wildlife score into the shelter experiment. If habitat affects success, its evidence and timing become mandatory.

**Artifact:** harvest → supply → delayed food access change → adaptation example, with an alternative that preserves the patch. Ecological interpretations still require factual verification before educational publication.

## Experiment integration — G17

**Needed:** explicit questions and criteria for joining shelter reasoning with water manipulation.

| Option | Benefit | Cost / risk |
|---|---|---|
| Keep A and B separate | Cleanest tests, small scope | Cannot demonstrate the combined game |
| Run separate tests, then one constrained combined scenario | Can examine whether water serves shelter/food needs | Integration may add cognitive load |
| Build combined loop immediately | Tests full fantasy sooner | Hard to identify which mechanic caused confusion |

**Recommend separate tests followed by a constrained integration proposal.** Require a specific relationship: a dam choice changes shelter access and competes with retaining food vegetation. More water is not automatically better.

**Artifact:** A/B/integration question matrix, including what each test cannot establish. Paper rules coherence is not evidence of satisfying water animation.

## Observing engagement — G18

**Needed:** evidence plan that distinguishes comprehension, interesting choices and interface friction.

| Option | Benefit | Cost / risk |
|---|---|---|
| Ask if players liked it | Low effort | Courtesy responses obscure problems |
| Observe choices, ask neutral follow-up, test contrasting reach | Connects behavior and reasoning | Requires consistent facilitator notes |
| Instrument a large quantitative study | Detailed behavior counts | Premature before rules are stable |

**Recommend observed paper sessions first**, after complete rules exist. Record alternatives considered, successful-path decisions, where explanations were needed, recovery, perceived fairness and transfer. Ask “What made you choose that?” after commitment; do not coach the choice. Record unknowns and facilitator interventions.

**Artifact:** one-page observation sheet and revision log. Report counts and examples; do not treat a small group as a precise audience estimate. Screen feel, touch controls and real pacing remain later playable-test questions.

## Campaign mastery — G19

**Needed later:** progression through reasoning, beyond a list of locations.

Options: standalone scenarios (simple, little continuity); short authored arc (controlled teaching and recombination); branching campaign (agency but more content).

**Recommend standalone paired scenarios initially**, then consider a short arc: identify access → compare exposure → allocate preparation → combine water and vegetation → recognize when to relocate. This is a proposed learning order, not a selected chapter list.

**Artifact:** chapter concept, prerequisite knowledge, new decision, reused rule and transfer challenge. Do not require a campaign to close the first scenario's gaps.

## Rewards and persistence — G20

**Needed later:** what the player values after success and what persists.

Options: journal entries as acknowledgement (small, optional); named optional accomplishments (clear goals, risk of grading experimentation); family/home continuity (attachment, greater state/content scope).

**Recommend optional journal acknowledgement first**, with no compulsory reading or collectible completion gate. Consider accomplishments only after ordinary play is enjoyable; avoid penalizing hints or first-attempt repairs.

**Artifact:** reward → player purpose → persistent state table and rules for replay/reset. Player journal knowledge and beaver competence remain distinct.

## Sandbox — G21

**Needed later:** mode purpose and stopping points.

Options: omit the mode (protect scope); replay scenarios without budgets (experimentation, may remove tradeoffs); dedicated landscape sandbox (ownership, significant content demand).

**Recommend deferring mode selection.** Scenario reset and reversible previews can support experimentation now. A future sandbox needs its own reason to play, feedback and save/reset boundaries; it is not automatically the campaign with victory removed.

**Artifact when revisited:** one-page mode brief specifying pleasures, constraints and optional goals.
